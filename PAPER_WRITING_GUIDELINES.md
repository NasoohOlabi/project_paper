# Shared paper-writing guidelines

Apply these rules when revising either language version. Use [ARABIC_WRITING_GUIDELINES.md](ARABIC_WRITING_GUIDELINES.md) for Arabic wording and [BILINGUAL_NOTES.md](BILINGUAL_NOTES.md) for the current revision coverage.

## Scope and application of author feedback

Apply this procedure whenever the author gives a correction or preference, even while reviewing one sentence or chapter.

1. Determine the scope from the instruction and its meaning. A requested sentence rewrite, figure adjustment, or example-specific correction is local. A terminology choice, prohibited expression, recurring writing principle, or correction to the method's definition applies wherever that concept occurs in the paper, unless the author limits it. The passage that prompted the note is an example, not automatically its boundary. A question or proposed alternative remains exploratory until the author adopts it.
2. Act immediately when the scope is clear. If it is genuinely unclear whether a note is local, language-wide, paper-wide, or project-wide, ask one concise scope question before making dependent changes. Continue work whose scope is already established. Do not repeatedly ask about a scope the author has already confirmed.
3. Record durable rules in their authoritative guide in the same change. Put Arabic wording rules in the Arabic guide and shared writing or method rules here. State the scope and any exceptions. Update an older rule when the author supersedes it; keep historical audit quotations as history. A local edit does not become a permanent global rule merely because it was accepted.
4. Apply a broad rule to every affected occurrence now, including earlier chapters, summaries, terminology, tables, captions, appendices, and figure labels. Inspect figure sources and visible image text as well as LaTeX prose; update and rebuild affected assets. For shared technical changes, update both languages. An Arabic vocabulary choice applies to the Arabic version, not to unrelated English terms or backend identifiers. Extend changes to presentations or other repositories only when the author's scope includes them.
5. Preserve protected content. Either abstract still requires explicit authorization for the specific edit. If propagation reaches protected content, request that authorization and identify the pending occurrence; do not silently exempt it or claim complete application. Preserve verbatim research prompts, quotations, and historical records unless the instruction includes changing them; explain any necessary exception.
6. Before finishing, search the entire applicable scope for the old wording, its grammatical variants, and equivalent descriptions of the corrected concept. Read each remaining match in context. For a structural or method correction, inspect related explanations and conclusions rather than relying on word replacement. Rebuild affected papers and inspect changed figures or layout. Report what scope was covered and any unresolved exceptions. Updating the guide or the currently open chapter alone does not complete a paper-wide instruction.

For example, “keep prompt in English” governs every Arabic description of model input, including appendix headings. “Rewrite this caption” changes that caption. “Explain the problem before the mechanism” governs relevant explanations throughout the paper without forcing every paragraph into one template.

## Shared writing and method rules

- Keep the evaluation roadmap and setup table distinct: the roadmap connects research questions, while the table identifies model roles, sampling controls, total attempts, payloads, and receiver inputs. Its arrows do not imply a common sample, a chronological experiment sequence, or universal success. Preserve experiment-specific settings; never fill missing historical values with current defaults. Label preparation, generation, checking, selection, and receiver costs by the operations actually measured. Exact dated artifact identities belong in the evidence records outside the manuscript.
- Attribute the literature synthesis to the author's SLR using its existing unpublished citation. Its 31-study denominator is the completed extraction set matched to the project's study table, not every candidate or later addition in the broader review. Do not transfer a different draft's totals or percentages without matching membership and classification. Keep watermarking evidence distinct from covert payload transmission and avoid inventing search dates or screening counts. Reconcile the source set and saved setup settings with `python scripts/build_experiment_setup_evidence.py`.

- Treat prompt variants as explicit experimental conditions, with their changed instructions, payload requirements, validation, sampling, and endpoints stated. Distinguish exploratory prompt ablation from a causal claim about one isolated instruction. Ordinary-writing controls without a target do not have a zero payload-recovery rate. In the saved 48-post prompt screen, "exact recovery" summary fields record sender candidate/topic validation, not complete independent message extraction. Keep its accepted-text judge subsets, first-draft snapshots, traced generation reruns, and later full-message archive experiment separate. Rebuild the shared numeric trace with `python scripts/build_prompt_ablation_evidence.py` after related edits.
- Keep the three research questions aligned across the English and Arabic introductions. Their explicit answers belong once at the end of evaluation, after the relevant evidence and before limitations, following the author-approved pattern in the supplied reference. Summarize the supported answer with selective results and references; do not repeat every experiment or copy the answer sequence into the conclusion. Keep the conclusion organized around contributions, implications, and future work.
- Explain important configuration values beside the mechanism they control. Identify whether a value follows a representation field, belongs to an implementation/resource preset, is required for shared interpretation, or was actually compared experimentally. State its purpose and tradeoff without inventing why the exact value was originally chosen or claiming a default is optimal. Give the rationale once; later experiment descriptions retain their actual values and refer back when needed. Keep topic-list size, preliminary retrieval-pool size, final prompt shortlist, candidates per generation, retries, and total carrier budget distinct. A stated sampling setting, including temperature zero, is not by itself proof of repeatability.
- Curate experiment descriptions around the research questions rather than an inventory of executions. Explain the question an experiment addresses, the finding or distinction it adds, and why the next part of the evaluation is needed. Use tables as reference maps supporting connected prose. Place diagnostic and component checks beside the question they clarify; avoid repeating settings, counts, or caveats already explained nearby. Describe a logical progression without inventing a historical causal sequence or implying that separate samples form one continuous experiment. Preserve enough selection and validation detail for readers to judge the evidence.
- Keep experiment groups distinct from datasets and independent replications. Document source selection, exclusions, payload origin, nested subsets, repeated posts, and paired policies before interpreting sample counts. Distinguish newly acquired posts from posts unused in generation; do not describe a development panel as untouched. Do not sum overlapping post sets, judge tasks, carriers, planning cells, or diagnostic cases into one sample size. Check saved recovery/archive post intersections with `python scripts/build_dataset_overlap_evidence.py`; do not infer disjoint historical comparison sets from different experiment names.
- Describe receiver validation at the level supported by the evidence. Credit unit/component checks, repeated-preparation checks, and complete-message experiments separately; do not call the receiver or an implemented component "untested" merely because one integrated deployment experiment remains. State the specific combination of acquisition, list construction, model calls, publication, and reception that a given experiment covers. Controlled substitutes validate the isolated operations, not unrestricted model or platform behavior; successful examples do not constitute a general correctness proof. Keep existing measured failures and experimental scope intact.
- Use **multi-comment mode** / **نمط التعليقات المتعددة** for transmitting one message through several carrier comments; a frame is one carrier comment. Give large-payload transport its own developed section. Keep single-comment selection separate from message-assembly framing, compression, and protection. Transport overhead belongs to the selected mode; multi-comment mode can still use its header when its complete stream happens to fit in one carrier. Never charge its header to each comment or automatically to the single-comment baseline.
- Explain transport fields from the actual implementation. The current 16-bit prefix is one fixed recognition pattern, not a catalogue of 65,536 formats; document only implemented parsing/encoding cases. The current control prefix contains marker, version, gamma-coded carrier count and prepared-payload length, followed by payload and a separate CRC. Its size varies with count/length. Explain exact scope, padding, and what the receiver already receives externally; do not claim every field or its width is indispensable or optimal. Compression identity and transport recognition are separate.
- For a recorded multi-comment example, show one whole message, each carrier's choices, message-level fields, CRC and final padding, actual receiver inputs, and complete-message extraction. Preserve source bytes, including whitespace. Keep sender validation distinct from receiver execution and recorded model outcomes distinct from a new offline codec replay. Reproduce the numeric trace with `scripts/build_multicomment_example.py` in the backend's existing Python environment; do not make new model calls merely to restate a saved example.
- Preserve the author's baseline-selection rationale: a comparator is needed to assess specific proposed improvements, and finding a directly comparable method was difficult because embedding channels, model access, shared inputs, and payload-growth mechanisms differ. ZLG is a runnable zero-shot reference with an official implementation, not proof that no closer precedents exist. Do not describe every methodological difference as a disadvantage to our method or assume that every metric must improve.
- Explain the scope cost of using a small local model for the comparison: the comment-quality experiment used the Qwen 3.5 9B family in both arms, leaving larger/API/reasoning-model flexibility untested. Do not assert identical quantization/runtimes, a historical context-window size without records, or a measured benefit from larger models. More distinguishable topics add bits logarithmically; more similar topics can increase identification and shortlist errors.
- Keep capacity accounting experiment-specific. The historical `capacity_matched` runner used per-sample budgets with byte rounding and payload excerpts; it did not establish exactly equal independently recovered useful payloads. The reported 16-bit average is neither a universal target nor ZLG's maximum. Compare longer token streams and additional comment carriers through whole-message recovery, total words, carrier count, failures, and preparation cost. Preserve the distinction between implemented framing support and evaluated real-platform delivery, including cross-post delivery.
- Keep prompt-imitation alignment beside standout results and its figure as a possible explanation, not an isolated causal finding. Do not extend it to suspicion AUROC. Preserve the small numerical AUROC difference and the fact that the paired raw-score test does not test the AUROC gap. Domain vocabulary may affect unigram KL/JSD alongside text length and sparsity; no domain-research ablation has established its causal effect.
- Explain historical receiver assistance concretely: our audit indices bypass choices the receiver should infer, while ZLG's token IDs and supplied length bypass token reconstruction and payload-end inference. These are limits of those comparisons, not inherent method disadvantages, and do not cover later independent archive experiments. Treat retry budgets and hardware as experimental resource settings; do not assert equal computational budgets or an isolated retry advantage without evidence. Keep baseline rationale, local result interpretation, and future extensions in their respective sections; run `python scripts/check_baseline_coverage.py` after related revisions.
- Do not use calendar dates, month names, timestamps, or dated artifact names as versions of our method, implementation, datasets, or experiments anywhere in either paper, including headings, tables, captions, appendices, and visible diagram text. Use a descriptive role consistently across both languages: initial configuration comparison / مقارنة الطريقتين في التجربة الأولى; comment-quality evaluation / تقييم جودة التعليقات; source-excerpt recovery / استخراج مقتطفات المصادر; independent-payload development / تطوير باستخدام حمولات مستقلة; archive reconstruction / إعادة البناء من الأرشيف. Use initial, revised, first, or second only when the order is clear. Explain the earlier design's relevant limitation and what the revision changes, including remaining tradeoffs; do not imply that a later implementation improves measured performance without evidence. Omit development history that does not help interpret the method or results. Preserve experimental populations, settings, results, and receiver-input distinctions. Keep exact artifact identifiers and their dates in provenance records outside the paper. This rule does not remove publication dates, bibliographic years, quoted research inputs, or timing values needed by the protocol. Run `python scripts/check_experiment_labels.py` after relevant revisions.
- Describe processing the same post twice as a development/research validation procedure. In deployment, the sender prepares its message and the receiver reconstructs the agreed inputs; the sender is not required to repeat preparation twice for every message. Keep the validation explanation in evaluation, with only a brief introduction reference where useful.
- Treat cache reuse as an implementation optimization to reduce repeated processing, service-quota use, and time on limited hardware, not as a requirement of the proposed method or the source of its intended reproducibility. Mention it only in implementation/resource details when needed. Distinguish a separate repeatability validation from processing the evaluation dataset: do not require every post to be processed twice, or call the dataset invalid merely because it was not. Describe the scope and outcome of verified validation evidence accurately, separately from message-extraction results.
- Present the final methodology in the present tense. Put configuration history and differences between evaluated runs in implementation or evaluation when they affect interpretation.
- Explain the problem before the mechanism. Introduce changing comments before the shared comment copy and changing web content before persistent and ephemeral document sets.
- Introduce components, parameter groups, and an ordered workflow before explaining its stages. Define the fixed helper-model setting once as a displayed `temperature = 0` equation; keep writing and topic-identification settings separate.
- Describe the general method before practical settings. Explain top-ranked search results without pagination argument names, provider workarounds, local-model format exclusions, or fetch-batch sizes.
- Cite OPPRESSION using `\cite{zillien2024oppression}` whenever it is named or its mechanisms are attributed in prose, tables, or captions. Cite the opening sentence beneath headings rather than the heading. Explain its codebooks, document pointers, and tree nodes before describing our simplified adaptation. Google Custom Search and model-generated queries are our implementation choices.
- Keep the source-text list, comment-topic list, and compression dictionary distinct. Explain topic extraction from each text, collection and ordering, and the quote, topic description, category, and source reference in each record.
- Use one ordered list for the reply-location and topic choices. Preserve the distinction between an invertible selection budget and audit-assisted reconstruction in the reported experiments.
- Use black-box linguistic steganography in English and the approved Arabic equivalent in Arabic. Define the term in the background and terminology; avoid repeating the full English phrase in Arabic running text.
- Use "copy of the post's comments" and «نسخة من تعليقات المنشور» for comments snapshots or dumps. Timing is implicit in the term and explained separately in the synchronization procedure.
- Keep each explanatory figure near its introduction. Use the approved source collection, topic mapping, and binary-selection diagrams, with equivalent examples in both languages. Each binary value must select the highlighted row.
- Port substantive revisions to both versions in the same change. Record coverage and Git provenance in the bilingual ledger. Preserve unrelated edits and do not edit either abstract without explicit authorization.
