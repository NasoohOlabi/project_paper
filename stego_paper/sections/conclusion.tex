\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work hides a payload in a threaded discussion by choosing a reply target and a comment topic, then asking an LLM to write an ordinary comment.
The available selection budget depends on distinguishable choices in the two lists; actual extraction also requires the receiver to identify the choices correctly.
The design does not insert bits into next-token sampling, and it does not need white-box logits.

An independent LLM-judge run on the 244-post unique-ZLG subset~\cite{lin2024zero} found ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} harder to pick in a same-thread lineup (28.3\% versus 51.6\% standout detection) and our method higher on register-fit (3.193 versus 2.791, paired sign-test $p=0.00374$).
The source campaign, run with one retry rather than the default six, succeeded on 2{,}543 of 4{,}044 attempts, all by audit-assisted reconstruction from sender-side metadata.
The initial configuration comparison, averaged over 100 posts, reported an available fixed-width selection budget of 15.14 bits/comment for our method versus 16.00 reported bits for ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero}, and GPT-2 perplexity of 68.15 versus 53.70 (ours versus ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero}), with 554 of 627 project-side attempts succeeding. These bit figures do not measure independently recovered project-side payload; the ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} reveal used sender token IDs and externally supplied payload length.
The two comparisons describe comment quality and generation success, using sender-side data to reconstruct the payload. The archive experiments instead tested message extraction after each party independently fetched the conversation and rebuilt its topic list in a local simulation. That simulation did not include posting comments on the platform or fully reconstructing web texts and model outputs. The reported results also did not test resistance to an adaptive steganalyst or to editing the text after publication.

An exploratory reply-chain probe removed a cross-thread topic mismatch on one frozen post and passed strict decoding on its first frame.
Planning the 64-bit payload required 19 frames, and only 13 of the first 25 posts fit within 20 frames.
This probe does not demonstrate complete payload recovery and shows the capacity cost of restricting topics to the selected reply chain.
The source-excerpt recovery experiment recovered 7 of 25 assigned messages exactly under shared frozen state and supplied carrier order. All 25 offline transport checks passed, but 13 receiver-failed messages contained classification confusion and five attempts failed generation. Compression reduced planned framed bits by 4.08\%; it did not resolve the linguistic selection failures.
The independent-payload Luna development check then stopped no-go with four exact recoveries among seven attempted messages and three posts unattempted. All attempted messages generated, but four wrong angle classifications prevented three complete recoveries. This result supports continued work on selection agreement before a larger campaign.
The later archive simulation independently reconstructed the existing thread-only extractive list and recovered \FreshDevSevenExact/\FreshAssigned{} development and \FreshHeldSevenExact/\FreshAssigned{} untouched messages with the anchored seven-attempt baseline. Concise selection reduced all-attempt words by \FreshDevSaving\% and \FreshHeldSaving\% with unchanged observed exact counts, but the pairwise quality judgments did not support improvement or preservation. We retain the anchored baseline and treat concision as an experimental efficiency tradeoff. These are local public-observation results, not actual Reddit delivery or full web/LLM reconstruction. Public discovery, bundle-level assessment, a fair baseline comparison, dedicated steganalysis and editing attacks remain necessary before stronger claims.

\label{par:baseline-interpretation}
ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} provided a runnable comparison on common posts, with different model-access requirements, information-carrying choices, and receiver inputs. Using a local model and the single-comment path restricted what this experiment could reveal about our model flexibility and larger-message delivery. Improvement is therefore tied to the measured criterion and configuration: some quality judgments favored our method, ZLG was harder to identify in standout detection, and suspicion AUROC values were close. These results do not quantify the benefit of a larger model or provide an overall ranking of message-extraction reliability.

Future evaluation can use larger or stronger-reasoning models while controlling context, topic count, and topic similarity. This would test whether API model flexibility improves generation or topic identification instead of assuming a gain from model size alone.

\paragraph{Messages distributed across posts.}
\label{par:cross-post-extension}
A larger payload can be distributed across comments on different posts, with separate context and lists for each post. The code supports splitting and reassembling bits across posts, but the reported comparisons did not evaluate this path in actual platform delivery. Extending deployment requires an agreement for identifying and ordering comments belonging to the same message, handling missing comments, and dealing with unavailable post inputs. Evaluation should account for complete-message recovery, preparation cost per post, and the naturalness of the comment group as well as each individual comment.

A further ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} comparison should use common frozen posts and settings and distinguish available selection budget, sent payload, and independently recovered message bits. Fluency, divergence, dedicated steganalysis, and editing attacks should be evaluated on the same data.
