\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work hides a payload in a threaded discussion by choosing a reply target and a comment topic, then asking an LLM to write an ordinary comment.
Capacity is whatever those two lists uniquely decode.
The design does not insert bits into next-token sampling, and it does not need white-box logits.

An independent LLM-judge run on the 244-post unique-ZLG subset found ZLG harder to pick in a same-thread lineup (28.3\% versus 51.6\% standout detection) and our method higher on register-fit (3.193 versus 2.791, paired sign-test $p=0.00374$).
The source campaign, run with one retry rather than the default six, succeeded on 2{,}543 of 4{,}044 LUCID attempts, all by audit-assisted reconstruction from sender-side metadata.
A historical 2026-07-30 configuration, averaged over 100 posts, had recoverable capacity 15.14 versus 16.00 bits/comment and GPT-2 perplexity 68.15 versus 53.70 (ours versus ZLG), with 554 of 627 project-side attempts succeeding.
Those two artifacts answer different questions.
Neither is a blind, capacity-matched ranking, and neither establishes resistance to an adaptive steganalyst.
Future work should freeze a symmetric manifest, score GPT-2 and divergence on that freeze, decode without sender audit files, and test dedicated steganalysis and editing attacks.
