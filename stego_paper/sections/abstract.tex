This paper proposes a black-box linguistic steganography workflow for threaded discussions that hides bits by choosing a reply target and a comment topic, then writing an ordinary comment.
The receiver is designed to recover those choices from a supplied thread snapshot and shared operational parameters, without relying on any hidden markup.
The method does not use invisible characters.
To assess generated comments, a separate language model evaluated 244 paired outputs on several criteria.
In the three-reply task, it chose our comment as the least human-like in 29.1\% of cases, compared with 47.5\% for ZLG, and rated our comments higher on register fit (3.193 versus 2.791).
In the ten-comment insertion task, it identified our comment more often than ZLG's (51.6\% versus 28.3\%).
These task-specific judgments do not establish steganographic security or independent payload recovery.
