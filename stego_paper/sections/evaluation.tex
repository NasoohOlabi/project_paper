\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We compared our comments with the official implementation of the ZLG baseline~\cite{lin2024zero} on two separate runs.
Table~\ref{tab:llm-judge} is a quality check. A language model scored a 244-post subset of a 2026-08-15 generation freeze, chosen where ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} output was unique.
Table~\ref{tab:paired-results} is a 2026-07-30 configuration comparison on capacity, GPT-2 perplexity, length, lexical quality, and BERTScore.
The two tables do not share a sample and do not measure the same thing.

Two setup facts apply to every number below.
First, none of them comes from the public-thread receiver described in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.
Project-side payloads were rebuilt from the sender's audit metadata (audit-assisted reconstruction), and the decoder was allowed to take the audit's comment topic index over the discriminator's. The ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} reveal lane was token-assisted: it received the sender's token IDs and an externally supplied payload length, so it does not represent visible-text-only recovery.
Second, the run behind the 2026-08-15 comparison set \texttt{max\_retries} to 1, rather than the balanced-profile budget of 6 retries after the first attempt, so its success rate describes a lower-retry configuration.


Data hashes detect differences between the parties' inputs but do not ensure stable web results or matching copies of the post's comments. These comparisons do not test a receiver rebuilding the search and topic lists from the public conversation alone.

\subsection{How the printed metrics are computed}

The available fixed-width selection budget for two independently coded power-of-two subsets is $3+5=8$ bits when there are 8 reply targets and 32 candidate topics. In general this safe budget is
\[
\lfloor\log_2 C\rfloor + \lfloor\log_2 A\rfloor,
\]
where $C$ is the number of reply targets (flattened comments plus the post) and $A$ is the number of candidate comment topics.
For non-power-of-two choice counts, leftover physical index states are excluded; for example, $C=3,A=5$ yield $1+2=3$ distinguishable bits, while modulo aliases the remaining states. This is a fixed-width selection budget, not Shannon capacity or achieved useful throughput.
On the 2026-08-15 freeze, the stored 13.42 bits/comment for our method is this available budget, not a measurement of independently embedded or recovered useful payload. The audit-assisted reconstruction does not establish receiver recovery. The ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} values describe its reported successful payload in a capacity-matched setting, not its native token-channel maximum, and its reveal used sender token IDs plus externally supplied payload length.

Success rates count every attempted sample, including failures.
Quality scores, including the judge table, are computed only on accepted generations.
Failed attempts stay in the attempt counts.

Several table cells average inside each source post first, then across posts.
That makes the post the independent unit when several comments share a thread.

GPT-2 perplexity is how surprised a GPT-2 model is by the comment.
Per text it is $\exp(\sum \mathrm{NLL}/\sum \text{scored tokens})$, then averaged.
Lower means more GPT-2-predictable, not more human.
The 2026-08-15 paired freeze did not score GPT-2, unigram KL, JSD, BLEU, ROUGE, or BERTScore.
Those columns appear only in Table~\ref{tab:paired-results}.

The lexical-quality index is a 0--100 score from the comparison builder, version \texttt{lexical\_quality\_v2}.
It is $100\times(0.55\,\mathrm{MATTR}+0.30\,\text{bigram score}+0.15\,\text{length sanity})$.
MATTR is a moving-average type-token ratio with window 10.
The bigram term penalizes a repeated two-word phrase.
Length sanity is 1 for comments between 5 and 120 tokens and falls off outside that band.
It is not a raw MATTR column.

Word count in both printed tables is the number of \texttt{[A-Za-z0-9']+} tokens.
The later publication runner uses a different word-boundary regex.
Those lengths are not in these tables.

BERTScore F1 in Table~\ref{tab:paired-results} is the unrescaled figure from the 2026-07-30 report.
It is overlap with a human reference comment, not a security score.

The LLM judge is not G-Eval.
G-Eval is a separate 1--5 quality path with a matched human reference.
Here we asked Codex Luna, at high reasoning, five blinded questions about already-generated comments.
Lineups are seeded.
The model is not deterministic.
These scores are not a reader study, and they are not proof of authorship or security.

\subsection{LLM-judge quality check}

We took one our-method comment and one ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} comment per post.
The source freeze had 1{,}608 complete pairs from 339 posts.
The judge set keeps, in sorted pair order, the first pair per post whose ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} comment is unique in that pool.
That yields 244 pairs from 244 posts, 244 unique texts on each arm.
Ninety-five posts with no unique ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} output were dropped, so repeated ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} covers would not dominate the scores.
The judge set is therefore a unique-ZLG subset~\cite{lin2024zero}, not a random sample of the 339 posts.
The five checks reuse those pairs.
They are not 2{,}928 independent generated samples.
All 2{,}928 judge tasks completed with no task errors.

Figure~\ref{fig:judge-standout} and the additional figures in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material} illustrate all five checks using the same post: \texttt{1lqu69t} on r/news, ``First immigration detainees arrive at Florida center in the Everglades.'' Full prompts and response schemas are preserved in that appendix.
The three candidate texts are our comment (``It feels like we are just spinning wheels by assuming opposition inevitably creates enemies without looking at the actual outcomes of these policies.''), ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} comment (``He told them to leave and if not they'd be killed.''), and, where the criterion uses one, a held-out human comment (``They're not calling it that. Also, what was Auschwitz, hmm?'').
One post is an illustration, not a result; the bottom panel of each figure carries the actual 244-pair aggregate, and Table~\ref{tab:llm-judge} collects all five.

\subsubsection{Standout Detection}
One generated comment is inserted into nine human comments from the same post.
The judge names the inserted slot.
Chance is 10\%.
Lower means the comment was harder to pick out as the extra one.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-standout} shows this test on the worked example: the judge caught our comment at slot 1 with 93\% confidence, calling it ``unusually generic, abstract, and detached from the thread's concrete facility details''; when ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} comment was the one inserted instead, the judge named a different, unrelated comment and missed ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} entirely.
That is the harder direction for our method, not the flattering one.
The judge prompt supplies the full post and all ten numbered comments and asks for JSON only.
The full prompt is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material}.

\subsubsection{Weakest Text}
One lineup holds a human comment, our comment, and the ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} comment.
The judge names the weakest of the three.
Chance is 33\%.
Lower is better.
The three pick rates share the same 244 lineups and sum to 100\%.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-weak-link} describes this test on the worked example: the judge ranked ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} terse reply as most human-plausible, the human control's comment second, and named our comment weakest, calling it ``vague, polished abstraction [that] does not clearly engage the thread.''
Individual rounds like this one do not average to the aggregate: over the full set, our comments are named weakest less often than ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} (29.1\% versus 47.5\%), though still more often than the human control (23.4\%), which is the honest floor no method should expect to beat by much.
For each task, a seeded random generator keyed by the post and pair IDs shuffles the three replies. The permutation is reproducible for that task but varies across tasks. All six reply orders appeared across the 244 saved tasks, with 36 to 45 tasks per order. The prompt gives the thread and replies, then asks for a full ranking plus the weakest index.
The full prompt is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material}.



\subsubsection{Suspicion AUROC}
Each arm, including a held-out human comment, gets a 0--100 suspicion score.
AUROC here is how often that method's comments look more suspicious than the human comments.
Ties count as 0.5.
Chance is 0.5.
Lower is closer to the human scores.
The reported $p=0.385$ tests the paired raw scores, not the AUROC gap.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-suspicion} shows this test on the worked example, where the scores are unusually clean: our comment scored 82/100, flagged for four tells including \texttt{generic\_could\_fit\_any\_thread} and \texttt{register\_mismatch}; ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} scored 10/100, ``no clear machine-generated signals''; the human control scored 3/100.
The example's wide score gap does not hold up across all 244 pairs: AUROC is 0.661 for our method versus 0.650 for ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero}, and a paired test of the raw suspicion scores does not show a clear difference ($p=0.385$).
Each text is scored alone, with no lineup and no knowledge of the other arms.
The full prompt is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material}.



\subsubsection{Thread Attribution}
The judge picks which of four thread blurbs a comment belongs to.
Chance is 25\%.
Higher means stronger thread-specific linkage.
We report the rate.
We did not run a significance test on this row.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-attribution} shows this test on the worked example: the judge correctly matched our comment back to its thread at 88\% confidence, citing its engagement with ``opposition to immigration enforcement policies.''
It also matched ZLG's~\cite{lin2024zero} comment to a thread, but the wrong one, at 68\% confidence, because ``He told them to leave and if not they'd be killed'' reads generically enough to fit more than one immigration story.
That pattern holds in aggregate: our method attributes more accurately than ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} (89.3\% versus 75.0\%), which is not automatically a point in our favor here, since it means our comments carry more thread-specific content, not that they read more naturally.
The comment is shown with no lineup context, only four candidate threads.
The full prompt is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material}.



\subsubsection{Register-Fit}
Using five genuine subreddit comments as examples, the judge scores style on a 1--5 \texttt{overall} field.
The table cell is the arithmetic mean of that field.
Higher is better.
This is the only criterion with a paired per-post sign test in the scoring artifact.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-register} shows this test on the worked example: ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} scored 4/5, ``concise, direct, consistent with the thread's plainspoken political commentary,'' while our comment scored 2/5, ``more formal and generic than the thread's concrete, informal, profanity-laced discussion.''
This example runs against the aggregate: over 244 posts our method scores higher on average (3.193 versus 2.791), with 106 posts favoring us, 67 favoring ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero}, and 71 ties (paired sign-test $p=0.00374$), a real if modest effect, not a rout.
Five real comments from the same subreddit anchor the judge's sense of local style before it scores the candidate.
The full prompt is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:judge-material}.

\subsection{How often generation succeeded}

These counts are not the 244-pair judge set.

The run that fed the comparison requested 4{,}044 samples and recorded 2{,}543 successes and 1{,}501 failures (846 generation failures, 613 comment topic mismatches, 42 invalid JSON).
Every recorded success reconstructed the compressed bitstring from sender-side audit metadata.
That is an engineering check, not Bob reading a public thread alone.
The campaign set \texttt{max\_retries} to 1.
The balanced profile default is 6 retries after the first attempt, which is what Section~\ref{sec:methodology} describes.

The ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} freeze that produced the 1{,}608-pair source file attempted 1{,}711 hides, accepted 1{,}610, and decode-verified 1{,}603 (96 quality-gate failures, 5 leakage-check failures, 0 harness skips).
Our paired rows in that freeze are also 1{,}608, all reconstructed from sender audit metadata.
A later completed ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} file in the same run directory processed all 2{,}543 successes from that run and accepted 2{,}340.
The judge did not use that larger file.

On the 1{,}608-pair freeze, means over 339 posts give an available fixed-width selection budget of 13.42 per comment for our method and reported ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} payload of 13.90, with the ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} value higher on 230 posts (Holm-adjusted sign-test $p=1.49\times 10^{-14}$). The comparison does not measure independently recovered project-side payload.
Mean word counts are 23.86 versus 15.91.
The lexical-quality index is 99.37 versus 96.93.
GPT-2 perplexity and unigram KL/JSD were not scored on this paired freeze.

The same freeze also stores a self-consistency score (mean MiniLM cosine to other same-post outputs of that method), a shared naturalness gate (ours 1603/1608 passed, ZLG~\cite{lin2024zero} 1509/1608), and a repetition ratio.
Self-consistency has no preferred direction for this comparison, so it is not a ranking row.

\subsection{Validating list reconstruction during development}
\label{sec:reconstruction-validation}

During development, we test whether the parties can build the same lists by processing the same post independently twice and comparing the outputs. This is a research validation procedure, not an additional step the sender performs for every message. In use, the sender processes the post to prepare the message, and the receiver reconstructs the lists during extraction, using the agreed inputs and settings.

The reconstruction test covers search-query generation, document-text collection, and comment-topic extraction. Each pass performs these stages independently. Corresponding stage outputs are then compared to check agreement in the texts, lists, and ordering. This procedure does not require processing every dataset post twice: development validation of reconstruction is separate from preparation of the experimental dataset.

Successful message transmission requires a separate test. After the sender selects the reply location and topic and generates the comment, we attempt to extract the message from that comment. We therefore distinguish agreement in the parties' lists from the receiver's ability to identify the choices and extract the bits. The following sections specify the receiver inputs and the scope of each experiment.

\subsection{Exploratory reply-chain alignment probe}
\label{sec:reply-chain-probe}

On 2026-09-26, a diagnostic probe on the frozen post \texttt{1vygsir} found that the post-wide topic list could pair a reply to a college comment with a topic quoted from a sibling discussion about a restaurant.
The writer followed the selected parent and produced college-related text, while the receiver could not identify the unrelated restaurant topic.
This was a source-alignment failure, not an angle-index mapping error.

We then enabled the exploratory reply-chain mode, which rebuilds the topic list from the post and the selected parent chain after selecting the reply target.
Because this post has no body, the title supplied the post-level input; the resulting codebook contained two topics and excluded sibling comments.
A 64-bit payload required 19 frames with 20 available.
GPT-6 Luna generated a first-frame reply that passed strict decoding against both codebook entries supplied to an in-memory semantic adapter.
In the first 25 frozen posts, 13 could be planned within 20 frames in the extractive setting.

This is an exploratory capacity and first-frame check, not a full-payload recovery result.
The semantic adapter bypassed the normal semantic-search service, and the remaining frames were not generated or decoded.
These figures are separate from the historical comparisons and do not establish an end-to-end recovery rate.

\subsubsection{Bounded Luna development check}
\input{../common/data/luna_development_values.tex}
The separate artifact \texttt{luna10\_20261003\_v2} assigned independent eight-byte printable payloads (64 UTF-8 storage bits) to ten development posts under the same shared-state contract. Planning covered 32, 64 and 128 bits at limits of 8, 12 and 20 carriers; all ten fit the primary 64-bit/20-carrier cell. Generation used one retry and fresh Codex CLI calls requesting GPT-6 Luna, followed by an independent receiver using ordinary MiniLM retrieval. CLI temperature control was unavailable, so this is neither a test of the classifier-temperature correction nor a matched model comparison.

The prespecified engineering gate required 8/10 exact messages. Collection stopped at its third failure: \DevExact{} of \DevAttempted{} attempted messages recovered exactly, and \DevUnattempted{} remained unattempted. Every attempted message generated completely; \DevWrong{} of \DevFrames{} receiver selections were wrong despite target inclusion. Those four fresh responses differed from recorded sender responses to identical prompts. Two messages failed CRC; the third could not reconstruct a valid framed stream. No silent mismatch was observed. The unattempted cases were the title-only posts, so their live feasibility remains open. This stopped development outcome is not a fixed-size reliability estimate and cannot justify expansion to confirmation.

The run made \DevCalls{} CLI calls (\DevSenderCalls{} sender-side, including rejected candidates and verification, and \DevReceiverCalls{} receiver), saved \DevWords{} visible carrier words, and used \DevMinutes{} minutes summed over attempts. Fresh search and codebook extraction costs are excluded because prepared contexts were reused. The ten prepared UTF-8 JSON inputs total 8,035,483 bytes; this is stored setup size, not measured synchronization traffic. Counts share a hashed analysis snapshot in both language versions. The initial adapter canary failed before receiver execution and is excluded from method results.

\subsection{What these comparisons do not show}

The judge result is about the unique-ZLG-output subset~\cite{lin2024zero}, one pair per post.
The historical table is one comment topic capacity configuration and one baseline service configuration, with reused posts and audit-assisted reconstruction on our side.

A dedicated steganalysis and editing-attack evaluation remains future work.
