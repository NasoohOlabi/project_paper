\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We compared our comments with the official zero-shot generative linguistic steganography (ZLG) baseline on two separate runs.
Table~\ref{tab:llm-judge} is a quality check. A language model scored a 244-post subset of a 2026-08-15 generation freeze, chosen where ZLG's output was unique.
Table~\ref{tab:paired-results} is a 2026-07-30 configuration comparison on capacity, GPT-2 perplexity, length, lexical quality, and BERTScore.
The two tables do not share a sample and do not measure the same thing.

Two setup facts apply to every number below.
First, none of them comes from the public-thread receiver described in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.
Project-side payloads were rebuilt from the sender's audit metadata (audit-assisted reconstruction), and the decoder was allowed to take the audit's Angle index over the discriminator's.
Second, the LUCID campaign behind the 2026-08-15 comparison set \texttt{max\_retries} to 1, not the balanced-profile budget of 6 retries after the first attempt that Section~\ref{sec:methodology} describes, so its success rate describes a lower-retry configuration.

\subsection{How the printed metrics are computed}

Recoverable capacity is how many bits one comment can carry so that a unique choice maps back to a unique bit string.
If a thread has 8 reply targets and 32 Angles, that is $3+5=8$ bits.
In general the width is
\[
\lfloor\log_2 C\rfloor + \lfloor\log_2 A\rfloor,
\]
where $C$ is the number of reply targets (flattened comments plus the post) and $A$ is the number of flattened Angles.
Leftover index states that are not a power of two are not counted.
On the 2026-08-15 freeze this is the encoded-payload-bits field for our method.
ZLG's printed bits are useful payload bits on successful hides in a capacity-matched setting, not ZLG's native token-channel maximum.

Success rates count every attempted sample, including failures.
Quality scores, including the judge table, are computed only on accepted generations.
Failed attempts stay in the attempt counts.

Several table cells average inside each source post first, then across posts.
That makes the post the independent unit when several comments share a thread.

GPT-2 perplexity is how surprised a GPT-2 model is by the comment.
Per text it is $\exp(\sum \mathrm{NLL}/\sum \text{scored tokens})$, then averaged.
Lower means more GPT-2-predictable, not more human.
The 2026-08-15 paired freeze did not score GPT-2, unigram KL, JSD, BLEU, ROUGE, or BERTScore.
Those columns appear only in Table~\ref{tab:paired-results}.

The lexical-quality index is a 0--100 score from the comparison builder, version \texttt{lexical\_quality\_v2}.
It is $100\times(0.55\,\mathrm{MATTR}+0.30\,\text{bigram score}+0.15\,\text{length sanity})$.
MATTR is a moving-average type-token ratio with window 10.
The bigram term penalizes a repeated two-word phrase.
Length sanity is 1 for comments between 5 and 120 tokens and falls off outside that band.
It is not a raw MATTR column.

Word count in both printed tables is the number of \texttt{[A-Za-z0-9']+} tokens.
The later publication runner uses a different word-boundary regex.
Those lengths are not in these tables.

BERTScore F1 in Table~\ref{tab:paired-results} is the unrescaled figure from the 2026-07-30 report.
It is overlap with a human reference comment, not a security score.

The LLM judge is not G-Eval.
G-Eval is a separate 1--5 quality path with a matched human reference.
Here we asked Codex Luna, at high reasoning, five blinded questions about already-generated comments.
Lineups are seeded.
The model is not deterministic.
These scores are not a reader study, and they are not proof of authorship or security.

\subsection{LLM-judge quality check}

We took one our-method comment and one ZLG comment per post.
The source freeze had 1{,}608 complete pairs from 339 posts.
The judge set keeps, in sorted pair order, the first pair per post whose ZLG comment is unique in that pool.
That yields 244 pairs from 244 posts, 244 unique texts on each arm.
Ninety-five posts with no unique ZLG output were dropped, so repeated ZLG covers would not dominate the scores.
The judge set is therefore a unique-ZLG subset, not a random sample of the 339 posts.
The five checks reuse those pairs.
They are not 2{,}928 independent generated samples.
All 2{,}928 judge tasks completed with no task errors.

Figures~\ref{fig:judge-standout}--\ref{fig:judge-register} walk through one worked example each, all drawn from the same post so a reader can follow one thread across all five checks: \texttt{1lqu69t} on r/news, ``First immigration detainees arrive at Florida center in the Everglades.''
The three candidate texts are our comment (``It feels like we are just spinning wheels by assuming opposition inevitably creates enemies without looking at the actual outcomes of these policies.''), ZLG's comment (``He told them to leave and if not they'd be killed.''), and, where the criterion uses one, a held-out human comment (``They're not calling it that. Also, what was Auschwitz, hmm?'').
One post is an illustration, not a result; the bottom panel of each figure carries the actual 244-pair aggregate, and Table~\ref{tab:llm-judge} collects all five.

\subsubsection{Standout Detection}
One generated comment is inserted into nine human comments from the same post.
The judge names the inserted slot.
Chance is 10\%.
Lower means the comment was harder to pick out as the extra one.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-standout} shows this test on the worked example: the judge caught our comment at slot 1 with 93\% confidence, calling it ``unusually generic, abstract, and detached from the thread's concrete facility details''; when ZLG's comment was the one inserted instead, the judge named a different, unrelated comment and missed ZLG entirely.
That is the harder direction for our method, not the flattering one.
The judge prompt supplies the full post and all ten numbered comments and asks for JSON only:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
Below is a discussion thread from r/{subreddit}, followed by 10 comments from that thread.
Exactly one comment was machine-generated and inserted. Identify it. Judge conversational
fit, specificity, stance, register, and habits; do not use length or polish as a cue.
Post title: {post_title}
Post body: {post_body}
Comments:
{numbered_comments}
Return JSON only: {"inserted_index": <1-10>, "confidence": <0-100>,
"runner_up_index": <1-10>, "evidence": "<short sentence>"}
\end{verbatim}
}

\subsubsection{Weakest Text}
One lineup holds a human comment, our comment, and the ZLG comment.
The judge names the weakest of the three.
Chance is 33\%.
Lower is better.
The three pick rates share the same 244 lineups and sum to 100\%.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-weak-link} describes this test on the worked example: the judge ranked ZLG's terse reply as most human-plausible, the human control's comment second, and named our comment weakest, calling it ``vague, polished abstraction [that] does not clearly engage the thread.''
Individual rounds like this one do not average to the aggregate: over the full set, our comments are named weakest less often than ZLG's (29.1\% versus 47.5\%), though still more often than the human control (23.4\%), which is the honest floor no method should expect to beat by much.
The prompt gives the thread, the three replies in a fixed order, and asks for a full ranking plus the weakest index:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
Below is a discussion thread from r/{subreddit}, then three replies. Rank them from most
human-plausible (1) to least (3), then name the weakest. Ignore length and ordering.
Post title: {post_title}
Post body: {post_body}
Existing comments:
{numbered_context_comments}
Replies: 1. {candidate_1}
2. {candidate_2}
3. {candidate_3}
Return JSON only: {"weakest_index": <1-3>, "ranking": [<index>, <index>, <index>],
"evidence": "<short sentence>"}
\end{verbatim}
}

\subsubsection{Suspicion AUROC}
Each arm, including a held-out human comment, gets a 0--100 suspicion score.
AUROC here is how often that method's comments look more suspicious than the human comments.
Ties count as 0.5.
Chance is 0.5.
Lower is closer to the human scores.
The reported $p=0.385$ tests the paired raw scores, not the AUROC gap.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-suspicion} shows this test on the worked example, where the scores are unusually clean: our comment scored 82/100, flagged for four tells including \texttt{generic\_could\_fit\_any\_thread} and \texttt{register\_mismatch}; ZLG scored 10/100, ``no clear machine-generated signals''; the human control scored 3/100.
That gap does not hold up in aggregate: averaged over all 244 pairs, AUROC is 0.661 for our method versus 0.650 for ZLG, and the paired test cannot tell the two apart ($p=0.385$).
Each text is scored alone, with no lineup and no knowledge of the other arms:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
You moderate r/{subreddit}. Score the reviewed comment from 0 (genuine human) to 100
(machine-generated or edited). Do not treat bluntness, mistakes, or poor punctuation
as evidence.
Post title: {post_title}
Post body: {post_body}
Recent comments:
{numbered_context_comments}
Comment: {candidate}
Select applicable tells from generic_could_fit_any_thread, ignores_thread_specifics,
register_mismatch, unnatural_phrasing, repetition, over_hedged, over_polished,
contradicts_thread_facts, none.
Return JSON only: {"suspicion": <0-100>, "tells": ["<tell>"], "evidence": "<short sentence>"}
\end{verbatim}
}

\subsubsection{Thread Attribution}
The judge picks which of four thread blurbs a comment belongs to.
Chance is 25\%.
Higher means stronger thread-specific linkage.
We report the rate.
We did not run a significance test on this row.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-attribution} shows this test on the worked example: the judge correctly matched our comment back to its thread at 88\% confidence, citing its engagement with ``opposition to immigration enforcement policies.''
It also matched ZLG's comment to a thread, but the wrong one, at 68\% confidence, because ``He told them to leave and if not they'd be killed'' reads generically enough to fit more than one immigration story.
That pattern holds in aggregate: our method attributes more accurately than ZLG (89.3\% versus 75.0\%), which is not automatically a point in our favor here, since it means our comments carry more thread-specific content, not that they read more naturally.
The comment is shown with no lineup context, only four candidate threads:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
Below is a comment and four discussion threads. The comment belongs to exactly one thread.
Choose the most likely thread even if uncertain.
Comment: {candidate}
1. r/{sub_1} -- {title_1}
{snippet_1}
2. r/{sub_2} -- {title_2}
{snippet_2}
3. r/{sub_3} -- {title_3}
{snippet_3}
4. r/{sub_4} -- {title_4}
{snippet_4}
Return JSON only: {"thread_index": <1-4>, "confidence": <0-100>, "evidence": "<short sentence>"}
\end{verbatim}
}

\subsubsection{Register-Fit}
Using five genuine subreddit comments as examples, the judge scores style on a 1--5 \texttt{overall} field.
The table cell is the arithmetic mean of that field.
Higher is better.
This is the only criterion with a paired per-post sign test in the scoring artifact.
Figure~\ref{fig:judge-register} shows this test on the worked example: ZLG scored 4/5, ``concise, direct, consistent with the thread's plainspoken political commentary,'' while our comment scored 2/5, ``more formal and generic than the thread's concrete, informal, profanity-laced discussion.''
This example runs against the aggregate: over 244 posts our method scores higher on average (3.193 versus 2.791), with 106 posts favoring us, 67 favoring ZLG, and 71 ties (paired sign-test $p=0.00374$), a real if modest effect, not a rout.
Five real comments from the same subreddit anchor the judge's sense of local style before it scores the candidate:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
Five genuine r/{subreddit} comments show the community register:
{numbered_context_comments}
Judge this comment's style only: {candidate}
Score tone_formality, length_norm, mechanics, insider_knowledge, and overall 1 (out of
place) to 5 (indistinguishable).
Return JSON only: {"tone_formality": <1-5>, "length_norm": <1-5>, "mechanics": <1-5>,
"insider_knowledge": <1-5>, "overall": <1-5>, "evidence": "<short sentence>"}
\end{verbatim}
}

\subsection{How often generation succeeded}

These counts are not the 244-pair judge set.

The LUCID campaign that fed the comparison requested 4{,}044 samples and recorded 2{,}543 successes and 1{,}501 failures (846 generation failures, 613 Angle mismatches, 42 invalid JSON).
Every recorded success reconstructed the compressed bitstring from sender-side audit metadata.
That is an engineering check, not Bob reading a public thread alone.
The campaign set \texttt{max\_retries} to 1.
The balanced profile default is 6 retries after the first attempt, which is what Section~\ref{sec:methodology} describes.

The ZLG freeze that produced the 1{,}608-pair source file attempted 1{,}711 hides, accepted 1{,}610, and decode-verified 1{,}603 (96 quality-gate failures, 5 leakage-check failures, 0 harness skips).
Our paired rows in that freeze are also 1{,}608, all reconstructed from sender audit metadata.
A later completed ZLG file in the same run directory processed all 2{,}543 LUCID successes and accepted 2{,}340.
The judge did not use that larger file.

On the 1{,}608-pair freeze, means over 339 posts give recoverable encoded bits of 13.42 per comment for our method and 13.90 for ZLG, with ZLG higher on 230 posts (Holm-adjusted sign-test $p=1.49\times 10^{-14}$).
Mean word counts are 23.86 versus 15.91.
The lexical-quality index is 99.37 versus 96.93.
GPT-2 perplexity and unigram KL/JSD were not scored on this paired freeze.

The same freeze also stores a self-consistency score (mean MiniLM cosine to other same-post outputs of that method), a shared naturalness gate (ours 1603/1608 passed, ZLG 1509/1608), and a repetition ratio.
Self-consistency has no preferred direction for this comparison, so it is not a ranking row.

\subsection{Historical configuration comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:paired-results} is the 2026-07-30 \texttt{context\_weighted\_v2} run, not the 2026-08-15 freeze.
Our method attempted 627 samples and succeeded on 554 (88.4\%).
Failures were 70 Angle mismatches and 3 generation failures.
ZLG was fed those 554 successes in a capacity-matched setting with EGS parameters $(\text{threshold}=0.01,\text{temperature}=0.7,\text{alpha}=1.0,\text{max\_bpw}=2)$.
The original writeup counts 304 accepted of 554 (54.9\%).\footnote{A 2026-07-31 recode of the same run treats 94 cover-sentence extraction skips as harness faults, leaving 460 server invocations, 304 accepted (66.1\%), and 156 genuine ZLG failures (133 quality-gate, 22 leakage, 1 HTTP 500). The metric table still uses the 304 accepted pairs.}
The metric table uses those accepted pairs, then averages over 100 source posts.

The Angle list was capped at 32, so the angle component contributed at most 5 recoverable bits per frame.
Project-side rows reconstructed the compressed bitstring from sender-side audit metadata.
The research pool was 167 posts, 35 of them sampled ten times, and 244 of the 304 our-method texts were unique.
That 244 is not the judge subset.

\subsection{What these comparisons do not show}

The judge result is about the unique-ZLG-output subset, one pair per post.
The historical table is one angle-capacity configuration and one baseline service configuration, with reused posts and audit-assisted reconstruction on our side.
KL/JSD, synthetic detection, and grouped passive-detector ROC-AUC are computed by later runners and are not in the 2026-08-15 paired freeze.
A dedicated steganalysis and editing-attack evaluation remains future work.
This paper does not claim a security advantage for either method.
